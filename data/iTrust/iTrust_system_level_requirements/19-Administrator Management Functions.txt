The healthcare personnel (HCP) is responsible for managing a variety of patient-related information within the system, including:

1. Patient Information: The HCP can enter and edit personal health details such as historical values, immunizations, office visit records (date, location, attending physician, diagnoses, medication, lab procedures, and family history). The HCP can also document the patient's Body Mass Index (BMI) and graph their height or weight data over the last three years, averaged by quarters.

2. Office Visits: The HCP can document office visit details including the patient's MID, visit date, hospital location, notes, medications (via NDC codes), lab procedures (via LOINC codes), diagnoses (via ICD-9CM codes), medical procedures (via CPT codes), immunizations (via CPT codes), and patient referrals. All changes are logged in the system for tracking.

3. Patient Data Management: The HCP can add or delete a patient's personal representative by typing their MID, and can enter new patient information (name, email, and an assigned MID). The HCP can also manage deceased patient data, including the cause of death (optional) and date of death.

4. Comprehensive Patient Reports: The HCP can view a comprehensive patient report that includes demographic data, health history, diagnoses, allergies, procedures, medications, office visits, relatives, and personal health representatives.

5. Demographic Data Entry: The HCP can enter or edit demographic information for both patients and healthcare personnel, except for security question and password data. HCPs can also upload patient photos as part of their records.

6. Security and Access: Patients and personal health representatives can enter or edit their own demographic information, including security question and answer (with confirmation). HCPs must verify a patient's MID before entering or editing data.

The system ensures that all events and changes made by the HCP are logged and tracked for accountability.
